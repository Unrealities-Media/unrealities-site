# R.I.P. FMV Season 1 plan

Oct 23, 2026 · Tom. Copy of the [Claude Doc](https://claude.ai/artifact/S5uJpTXWSzrumW1AxagB1G). Superseded by [channel-plan.md](channel-plan.md) (strategy v2, Season Zero first); kept for the episode groupings and the Content ID plan.

Season 1 of R.I.P. FMV covers every FMV-driven Sega CD game in 15 episodes: a pilot, two launch deep dives, ten grouped episodes and a collector's finale, with a bonus roundup of the cutscene games. The plan keeps the R.I.P. branding, titles episodes by subject rather than number, and puts the index on screen in every one.

## Scope: the 45, not the 67

Recommendation: Season 1 promises every Sega CD game where video drives the play, which in the index is FMV score 7 or higher. That is 45 obtainable games, so "The 39" becomes "The 45". The other 22 index titles (cutscenes, sports intros, game shows) get one bonus roundup so the running total still reaches 67.

| Option | Games | CIB to collect | What it promises on screen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FMV score 9–10 only | 39 | $1,969 | "Every game that is basically a movie" |
| **FMV score 7+ (recommended)** | **45** | **$2,231** | "Every FMV-driven Sega CD game" |
| Everything in the index | 67 | $3,319 | "Every Sega CD game with video in it" |

The 7+ line is the cleanest promise because it matches the database's own definition (video drives most play) and keeps Panic!, Iron Helix, Mansion of Hidden Souls, MegaRace and Power Rangers, which your original list wanted. It drops only two of your original 39: The Lawnmower Man (score 5) and NFL's Greatest Teams (score 3), both of which move to the bonus roundup. Maximum Surge is listed as not obtainable, so it appears as a story beat, not a played game.

Prices are CIB from PriceCharting as of 2026-10-20; Sherlock Holmes Vol. III (Japan only) and the Penn & Teller prototype have no price and are not in the totals.

## Episode notes

Every game gets the same index card on screen when it is introduced: `title`, `year`, `developer`, `category`, `fmv` score, `priceCib` with `priceDate`, and `cidRisk`. The fields listed per episode below are the extra ones that episode's story needs. Each episode ends with the running total of games catalogued (this episode's count, then the index total: 67 Sega CD, 212 across all systems) and a spoken pointer to the next episode.

### S1E01 Sewer Shark

- Title: The game 2 million people got free with their Sega CD | R.I.P. FMV
- Hook: a guy named Ghost yells at you in a sewer for an hour, and it was directed by the man who did the effects for Star Wars.
- Extra fields: `cast` (Robert Costanzo), `trivia` (John Dykstra; Hasbro Control-Vision leftover; pack-in from 1993), `difficulty`, `modern` (3DO port only).
- Loose end: ask viewers which bundle they got (the pack-in changed over the console's life).

### S1E02 Night Trap

- Title: Night Trap was never the game Congress thought it was | R.I.P. FMV
- Hook: the Senate hearings called it violence against women; it is a slow surveillance puzzle about trap timings.
- Extra fields: `cast` (Dana Plato), `trivia` (shot 1987, $1.5M, pulled 1993), `x32` (the 32X CD version), `cidTrigger` (the theme song), `modern` (25th Anniversary release), `sega16` 3/10 vs `mobyCritic` 67%.
- Loose end: the theme song's claim status after upload goes in the next episode's cold open.

### S1E03 The laserdisc ports

- Title: Dragon's Lair on Sega CD is the worst version of the most famous FMV game | R.I.P. FMV
- Hook: six 1980s arcade laserdisc games squeezed into 64 colours, and the forgotten Data East one is the best of them.
- Order inside: Cobra Command, Road Avenger, Time Gal, Revenge of the Ninja, then Dragon's Lair and Space Ace as the fall.
- Extra fields: `alt` (Thunder Storm FX), `jp`/`na`/`eu` flags (Time Gal and Revenge of the Ninja never left Japan), `priceCib` spread ($38 Dragon's Lair to $195 Time Gal), `modern`.
- Loose end: the Dragon's Lair PAL box variant is flagged "verify" in the index.

### S1E04 Digital Pictures after Night Trap

- Title: Corey Haim, Debbie Harry and a drill sergeant: the Night Trap sequel | R.I.P. FMV
- Hook: Digital Pictures spent three years remaking Night Trap with better casts, and Double Switch is the one that works.
- Deep dive: Double Switch. Fast pass: Ground Zero Texas (dir. Dwight H. Little), Prize Fighter, Corpse Killer (Vincent Schiavelli), Supreme Warrior.
- Extra fields: `cast`, `endings`, `x32` (Corpse Killer and Supreme Warrior have 32X CD versions), `modern` (the 25th Anniversary and Nuclear Edition re-releases).
- Loose end: Prize Fighter has no cheats or codes on file; ask if anyone has the noir intro's source.

### S1E05 Make My Video

- Title: The four worst games on the Sega CD are all the same game | R.I.P. FMV
- Hook: Sony thought kids wanted to edit music videos on a console, and shipped it four times in one year.
- Extra fields: `cidTrigger` (the full track lists), `mobyCritic` (28% for Marky Mark), `difficulty` ("None"), `length` (30 min each).
- Loose end: which of the four is the rarest; CIB prices run $21 to $38 and the index has no sales counts.

### S1E06 The lost Digital Pictures games

- Title: Digital Pictures kept releasing games six years after it died | R.I.P. FMV
- Hook: Bug Blasters was shelved in 1995, sold in 2001 and starred at AGDQ 2026's Awful Block; it is now one of the most expensive discs on the system.
- Extra fields: `status` (Released post-cancellation, NOT obtainable), `priceCib` ($192 for Bug Blasters, none for Citizen X), `cast` for Maximum Surge (Yasmine Bleeth, Walter Koenig), `difficulty` ("barely finishable on real hardware").
- Loose end: Bug Blasters and Citizen X have no critic scores in the index; collect viewer ratings.

### S1E07 American Laser Games

- Title: Four arcade light-gun games on a console with a terrible light gun | R.I.P. FMV
- Hook: the Menacer, the Justifier or the Sega Mouse; which one makes Mad Dog McCree playable?
- Extra fields: `peripherals` (all four support Controller, Mouse, Menacer, Justifier), `priceCib` (Mad Dog II $136, Crime Patrol $144 vs Mad Dog McCree $31), `modern` (the Remastered packs), `endings` for Who Shot Johnny Rock? (the randomised killer).
- Loose end: Crime Patrol and Johnny Rock have no MobyGames score for this platform.

### S1E08 Sega's own FMV studio

- Title: Sega made its own Top Gun game and nobody remembers it | R.I.P. FMV
- Hook: Sega Multimedia Studio and The Code Monkeys made five FMV games in two years, and the last one, Wirehead, is the best FMV game on the console.
- Sidebar: the 32X CD question. Fahrenheit ships both discs in one box, so it is the side-by-side comparison; Surgical Strike's 32X version only came out in Brazil.
- Extra fields: `x32`, `discs`, `variants`, `save` (Fahrenheit's is "Unknown, verify"), `cheats`.
- Loose end: Tomcat Alley and Midnight Raiders mission-select codes are flagged "verify"; ask viewers for confirmed inputs.

### S1E09 Power Rangers and Kamen Rider

- Title: Sega turned Power Rangers into a fighting game by cutting up the TV show | R.I.P. FMV
- Hook: the same engine, built first for the Kamen Rider ZO movie in Japan, then rebuilt around Season 1 of Power Rangers.
- Extra fields: `cidTrigger` (TV series footage and theme; film footage), `cast` (original MMPR cast; Kou Domon), `jp`/`na` flags.
- Loose end: Kamen Rider ZO's "difficulty settings only" cheat note is unverified.

### S1E10 The CG rail shooters

- Title: Rebel Assault sold a million CD-ROM drives, then came to the Sega CD in 64 colours | R.I.P. FMV
- Hook: pre-rendered video pretending to be a 3D game, from LucasArts, Psygnosis and a Japanese knock-off with a giant mech.
- Lead with Novastorm (the hardest, $140 CIB), then Microcosm, A/X-101, Loadstar (Ned Beatty), and Rebel Assault last and short. Silpheed cameo: same trick, score 6, outside the 45.
- Extra fields: `fmvType` (pre-rendered CG), `difficulty`, `cast` (Ned Beatty, Barry Primus), `cidTrigger` for Rebel Assault.
- Loose end: Novastorm's level-select code is flagged "verify".

### S1E11 The detectives

- Title: The best Sega CD FMV game costs $10 and nobody bought it | R.I.P. FMV
- Hook: Sherlock Holmes: Consulting Detective is still a good detective game, and Vol. III never left Japan.
- Deep dive: Sherlock Holmes Vol. I (Peter Farley, the 1981 board game). Fast pass: Vol. II ("The Tin Soldier"), Vol. III (English video, Japanese menus), Dracula Unleashed (the timetable puzzle), Iron Helix, Mansion of Hidden Souls.
- Extra fields: `length` (3–5 hr each), `peripherals` (Sega Mouse), `endings`, `modern` (PC and iOS versions on sale), `mobyCritic` (71% for Iron Helix and Mansion, the highest in the season).
- Loose end: Vol. III has no price in the index; ask importers what they paid.

### S1E12 The ones nobody should play (and Desert Bus)

- Title: Desert Bus was designed to annoy Janet Reno | R.I.P. FMV
- Hook: Penn & Teller's Smoke and Mirrors leaked 15 years after its publisher folded, and it is the most-played unreleased game on the system.
- Deep dive: Penn & Teller (Prototype dumped). Fast pass: Panic! (every button does something ridiculous), Kids on Site, Slam City with Scottie Pippen (the year the Bulls had no Jordan), MegaRace (Lance Boyle).
- Extra fields: `status` (Prototype dumped), `trivia` (Absolute Entertainment, Frank Cifaldi's 2010 leak), `modern` (Panic!'s PS2 remake), `cast` (Scottie Pippen, Christian Erickson).
- Loose end: the Lou Reed cameo in Smoke and Mirrors is flagged "verify".

### S1E13 The collector's verdict

- Title: What every Sega CD FMV game costs in 2026 | R.I.P. FMV
- Hook: the complete set of 45 is $2,231 CIB, and seven discs are half of it.
- Content: the 45 ranked by your own play (the tracker's `played` and `episode` fields), then the cost ladder, the seven price carriers, the five not worth it, and the season's open corrections read out.
- Extra fields: `priceLoose` vs `priceCib`, `priceDate`, `sega16` vs `mobyCritic` where they disagree, `urlPriceCharting` in the description.
- Loose end: the whole corrections list, with a link to the index.

### S1E14 Bonus: the 22 with a little bit of video

- Title: Sonic CD, Road Rash and the Sega CD games that used video as a garnish | R.I.P. FMV
- Hook: the console's best-selling game has 90 seconds of video; the most expensive one (ESPN NBA Hangtime '95, $268 CIB) has two.
- Extra fields: `fmv` score 1–6 on every card, `category` (Cutscenes only, Sports w/ FMV, Game show), `priceCib` extremes.
- Loose end: NFL's Greatest Teams save type, Cliffhanger clip count and Trivial Pursuit's publisher are all "verify" in the index.

## Port comparison strand

One game per episode works when the game has several versions to compare, and the laserdisc ports and light-gun games are the only Sega CD titles where that is true. Recommendation: run it as a strand inside Season 1 rather than making the season about it. Three of the Sega CD episodes above (E03 laserdisc ports, E07 American Laser Games, and the Night Trap dive) become version-comparison episodes, and any game below can later get a standalone "every version" video that links back into the season. As a whole season it would lose the Sega CD story, and six laserdisc games with the same 20-minute structure get repetitive fast.

### Episode template: every version of one game

1. Cold open: the same scene from the arcade original and the worst port, side by side, 20 seconds.
2. The original: year, format (laserdisc, 35mm, live action), how play worked in the arcade.
3. The version ladder, oldest to newest: for each, the video window size, colours, frame rate, how prompts are shown, input (controller, light gun, mouse), load pauses, what was cut. Cards from the index: `fmv`, `year`, `peripherals`, `priceCib`.
4. The verdict: which version to play today, which to collect, and what the `modern` field says is on sale now.
5. Loose end: a version you could not get hold of (Jaguar CD, LaserActive, Japan-only sets), so viewers fill it in.

## Packaging

The research is firm on one point: a stranger clicks the game and the hook, not an episode number. So the brand moves from the front of the title to the end, and the season numbering lives in the thumbnail corner, the description and the playlist.

| Element | Your original | Season 1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube title | `R.I.P. FMV S1E02 - Night Trap (Sega CD)` | `Night Trap was never the game Congress thought it was \| R.I.P. FMV` |
| Thumbnail badge | `R.I.P. FMV` | `R.I.P. FMV` (unchanged, top-left, same place every episode) |
| Thumbnail strip | `Sega CD - S1` | `SEGA CD · S1` with the episode number small at the right end (`E02`) |
| Thumbnail image |  | one face or one frame from the game, three words of text at most, no repeat of the title's words |
| Description, line 1 |  | `R.I.P. FMV S1E02. Sega CD, 1992, Digital Pictures. FMV score 10. Full index: <site link>` |
| Playlist |  | `R.I.P. FMV: Season 1, Sega CD` in episode order, autoplay on |
| First 30 seconds |  | the payoff clip and the episode's question before any logo or intro |

Keep the title under about 70 characters so the hook survives on phones; the titles in the episode notes are working titles and most need trimming. Test the top three episodes (Night Trap, Sewer Shark, Make My Video) with YouTube's Test & Compare, which picks by watch time share, before settling the pattern.

The index itself is the recurring prop: open the site on screen when each game is introduced, and show the tracker ticking from played to uploaded at the end of each episode.

## Pilot outline

Working title: Sega put a VCR inside a games console and 45 games came out | R.I.P. FMV. Length 8 to 10 minutes; it exists to explain the premise once so every later episode can skip it.

1. Cold open (0:00–0:30): the strangest ten seconds from five games back to back (Sewer Shark, Make My Video, Panic!, Mad Dog McCree, Desert Bus), then the question: why did anyone think this was the future?
2. What FMV is (0:30–2:30): streamed video off a single-speed CD, the video window, 64 colours, why it looks the way it does. Field on screen: `fmvType` and the `fmv` score scale from 1 to 10.
3. The bet (2:30–5:00): Hasbro's cancelled Control-Vision, how Night Trap and Sewer Shark were filmed in 1987 for a console that never shipped, and how Sega CD inherited them in 1992. The Senate hearings in one minute, saved for E02.
4. The promise (5:00–7:00): the index on screen. 45 FMV-driven games, 1992 to 1995 plus two post-cancellation releases, $2,231 to collect CIB. The categories as the season's map: laserdisc ports, Digital Pictures, American Laser Games, Sega's own, CG shooters, detectives, oddities.
5. The rules (7:00–8:30): every game played on original hardware, every card from the database, every episode ending with the running total, and viewers' corrections going back into the index.
6. Close (8:30–10:00): running total 0 of 45, index total 212, and the spoken pointer to E01 Sewer Shark.
