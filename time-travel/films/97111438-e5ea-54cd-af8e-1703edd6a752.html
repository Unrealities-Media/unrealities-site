The Beast

Bertrand Bonello · 146 minutes

In the near future where emotions have become a threat, Gabrielle finally decides to purify her DNA in a machine that will immerse her in her past lives and rid her of any strong feelings. She then meets Louis and feels a powerful connection, as if she had known him forever.

Why the archive makes this assessment (spoilers)

In 2044, where an AI-run society treats emotion as a threat, Gabrielle submits to a DNA purification that immerses her in her past lives so their traumas can be erased. The whole film is built from those immersions, intercutting her 1910 Paris life, ending in the Great Flood, and her 2014 Los Angeles life, with Louis returning in every era, the same dread of catastrophe carried across lives, and glitches as the periods bleed into each other. The machine-mediated reliving of other eras shapes the entire narrative, which lifts it well above its provisional 5. It stops short of Mr. Nobody's 8 because these are past incarnations relived from within rather than travel or the same life revisited, and nothing in the past can be changed.

A purification procedure brings memories of past incarnations. The distributor describes recollection rather than intervention or travel; whether this exceeds reincarnation and memory alone remains uncertain.

How Titan works

Titan combines the archive’s quality, audience and legacy base with the significance of time travel. It is an index, not a score out of 100, and is distinct from the FMV archive’s scoring model.

Titan = base × [0.20 + 0.80 × (time-travel score ÷ 10)²]
