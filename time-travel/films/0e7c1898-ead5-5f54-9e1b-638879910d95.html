A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court

Tay Garnett · 106 minutes

A bump on the head sends Hank Martin, 1905 auto mechanic, to Arthurian England, 528 A.D., where he is befriended by Sir Sagramore le Desirous and gains power by judicious use of technology. He and Alisande, the King's niece, fall in love at first sight, which draws unwelcome attention from her fiancée Sir Lancelot; but worse trouble befalls when Hank meddles in the kingdom's politics.

Catalog assessment: questionable
A head injury initiates the medieval adventure, but the film's dream framing makes literal displacement uncertain; retain for boundary review.
Time-travel significance
Told in 1912 to Lord Pendragon at Camelot's ruins, the story follows the mechanic Hank Martin, knocked out in a storm, who wakes in Camelot in 528. Almost the whole film is his stay there: dubbed Sir Boss, he wins standing through modern know-how, falls for the king's niece Alisande and meddles in the kingdom's politics, until he is shot and returns to 1912 to meet her lookalike. The displacement drives the story, like Black Knight or The Visitors. It stays at 7 because it is a single fish-out-of-water trip with no temporal mechanics or lasting consequences, and the knock-out framing leaves it possibly a dream.
