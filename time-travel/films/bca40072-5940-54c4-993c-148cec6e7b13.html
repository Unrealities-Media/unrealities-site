Catalog assessment: included
Eloise experiences vivid psychic visions of a woman's life in 1960s Soho rather than ordinary memories or physical travel.
Time-travel significance
Each night Ellie slips into 1960s Soho and lives Sandie's life, seeing herself as Sandie in every mirror, and those nights shape her days: she remakes her look, follows Sandie's rise and abuse, and witnesses what she takes to be Sandie's murder. In the second half the past bleeds into the present as faceless men from that era haunt her, and her investigation into what really happened decades ago drives the plot to the reveal that Sandie is still alive as her landlady. The past-in-the-present premise drives much of the story, so it scores like Arrival or the Christmas Carol visions rather than a single episode. It stops at 7 because Ellie observes and relives rather than travels or changes the past, and the film frames it as psychic sensitivity and haunting.
