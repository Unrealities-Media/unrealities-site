# What the archives are for

Primary purpose: help a curious visitor choose what to explore and understand why it belongs in the archive. Release and source data support that decision; record count is not a success measure.

## Three tasks each archive must support

1. **Find a starting point.** Give visitors a small number of intelligible questions or interests. Explain how a result set was selected. Keep the complete catalog available without making it the only entrance.
2. **Understand the significance.** Put the interpretation near the beginning of a guide. Distinguish a sourced fact, an editorial judgment, and uncertainty. Explain what a score measures instead of asking visitors to infer meaning from a number. Do not imply that an automated score predicts enjoyment or that a research assessment proves a film was watched.
3. **Choose a next step.** Link to supporting research, related classifications, or edition comparisons. A version preference needs a reason and its limitations. Where evidence does not establish a best release, say so and expose the specifications that help narrow a choice. Unknown is not absent; a listed edition is not proof of current availability.

## Editorial gate for updates

- A featured item or pathway must answer a visitor question, explain its inclusion, and lead somewhere useful.
- Film significance labels must follow Movie Archivist's documented rubric. They describe the role of temporal events, not film quality.
- Titan is one research signal. Preserve partial/provisional labels and explain its limits wherever ranking becomes a primary route.
- Keep plot spoilers behind an explicit disclosure. Put source links close enough to find from the assessment.
- Do not invent a "best edition" from resolution alone. Comparative judgments require edition-specific evidence.
- Remove repeated information and collapse specialist detail when it interrupts discovery.

## Validation

Automated tests check that the central-time-travel pathway selects included, high-confidence films with significance 7–10; uncertainty and missing scores keep their meaning; and the browsing controls work at mobile widths. These tests establish functional behavior, not audience usefulness.

For a human usability session, ask a new visitor to find one film/game they want to explore, explain why it interests them, identify one uncertainty, and find a sensible next step. Observe wrong turns and whether they can answer without knowing Titan or the catalog vocabulary. Revise the page around those failures. Do not count passing layout tests or a larger database as evidence that this task succeeded.
